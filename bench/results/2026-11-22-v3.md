## What ponytail changed

D3 is D2 with each worker started as `yap spawn --skills ponytail`. Pass rate 67% against 72% (-6 pts, over 18 and 18 runs, so one run is 6 pts). Median lines added 107 against 136 (-21%), wall-clock 88s against 86s (+3%), cost $0.23 against $0.23 (-2%), tokens +6%.

## Where each setup lost

**A2** (subagents, trusting (Task tool, features as files)) lost on pass rate (67% against D2's 72%), cost ($0.24 median against D3's $0.23). It failed 6 of 18 runs: 6 with one feature passing and the other not; pairs 56[1,5], 27[3,4]. Nobody would have had to step in.

**D2** (commander on Haiku, pointing (yap spawn)) lost on cost ($0.23 median against D3's $0.23), wall-clock (86s median against A2's 73s). It failed 5 of 18 runs: 5 with one feature passing and the other not; pairs 56[1,5], 27[3,4]. Nobody would have had to step in.

**D3** (D2 with lean ponytail workers (yap spawn --skills ponytail)) lost on pass rate (67% against D2's 72%), wall-clock (88s median against A2's 73s). It failed 6 of 18 runs: 6 with one feature passing and the other not; pairs 56[1,5], 27[3,4]. Nobody would have had to step in.


## Reading (hand-written, from the rows and the log)

The setups are a tie on pass rate. Every failure in all three is feature 1 of two pairs. 27[3,4] fails in every run of every setup in v1, v2 and v3 (18/18 here). 56[1,5] fails on `TestMethodNotAllowed/Unregistered_Method` in 8 of 9 runs. The whole spread, A2 67%, D2 72%, D3 67%, is D2 passing 56[1,5] once. One run is 6 points. Nothing here separates the architectures on correctness.

A2's trust rule did what the brief expected. Its tail fell from v2 A's 33.5 s to 4.0 s. Median wall fell from 107 s to 73 s, cost from $0.30 to $0.24, tokens by 100k, and the pass rate held at 67%. The 33 s in v2 was the prompt, not the architecture. A2 is now the fastest setup.

D2's changes bought cost, not time. Haiku and pointing cut the commander's plan phase from 12.7 s to 7.5 s and median cost from $0.28 to $0.23. The commander read more tokens (80k against v2 D's 54k), but at Haiku's price. Wall-clock went up 3 s anyway: the work phase grew from 66 s to 74 s, because each worker now reads its feature file itself. The commander still pays about 12 s to start each spawned session (the `start` column), which A2's in-process subagents don't pay.

Ponytail barely moved anything. D3's patches are 21% smaller (median 107 lines against 136). Pass rate went from 72% to 67%, cost and wall-clock are within 3%, and tokens rose 6%. That's one 56[1,5] run, and noise in the rest. The skill loaded (its SessionStart hook printed `PONYTAIL MODE ACTIVE — level: lite`), but no worker invoked it as a Skill. These features are already small, so there was little to cut.

Lines added were first counted from the raw patch. Agents on Windows rewrite whole files between CRLF and LF, which inflated 30 of 54 rows by up to 34× (mux.go +530 for a 24-line change). The column is now counted with `git diff --numstat --ignore-cr-at-eol`, and the 54 rows were recounted from their run directories. Pass/fail is unaffected, because scoring applies patches with `--ignore-whitespace`.
